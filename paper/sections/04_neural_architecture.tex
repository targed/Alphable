\section{Neural Value Network and Cross-Attention Architecture}
\label{sec:neural_architecture}

In classical Scrabble engines, board state evaluation is completely decoupled from rack leave evaluation: tabular leave values (such as KLV and KLV2) assess the quality of unplayed letters in isolation, blind to whether the board presents open triple-word lanes, constricted clusters, or accessible vowel hooks. To resolve this structural deficiency, \algo\ introduces a unified deep neural value network that jointly conditions board topology on rack composition.

\subsection{Input Tensor Representation}

The input to the \algo\ value network consists of two coordinated representations: a multi-channel spatial tensor representing the board grid and a discrete token sequence representing the rack. 

The spatial board state is encoded as a tensor $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{board}} \in \mathbb{R}^{28 \times 15 \times 15}$. Channels $0$ through $25$ correspond to binary one-hot indicator planes for the English alphabet characters $\text{A}$ through $\text{Z}$, recording the tile letter placed at each square. Channel $26$ provides a binary indicator plane for blank tiles, which designate valid words on the board but contribute zero nominal letter score. Channel $27$ contains a normalized premium multiplier grid that encodes the static geometry of Double Letter ($\times 1.2$), Triple Letter ($\times 1.5$), Double Word ($\times 2.0$), and Triple Word ($\times 3.0$) coordinates. 

Complementing the spatial tensor, the player's unplayed rack tiles are encoded as a discrete token sequence $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{rack}} \in \mathbb{Z}^7$. Each rack tile token is mapped through a learned embedding table into a dense $d = 64$-dimensional vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{rack}} = \mathrm{Embedding}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{rack}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 64}
\end{equation}
Empty rack slots, which arise when the tile bag is exhausted and fewer than seven tiles remain, are represented by a reserved padding token.

\subsection{Cross-Attention Backbone Architecture}

The dual inputs are processed through a hybrid convolutional and cross-attentive backbone, depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}. The spatial board tensor $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{board}}$ is initially processed by a $7 \times 7$ convolutional layer with $64$ filters, unit stride, and padding of $3$, followed by Batch Normalization and a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation. The resulting activations traverse four residual blocks modeled on the ResNet-18 architecture, extracting high-level spatial and combinatorial features into a tensor $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{board}} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 15 \times 15}$.

To enable direct interaction between the rack and spatial features, $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{board}}$ is flattened across its spatial dimensions into a sequence of $225$ spatial visual tokens:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{board}}, \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{board}} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{board}}^{\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{225 \times 64}
\end{equation}
We formulate a multi-head cross-attention mechanism with $h = 4$ heads, where the seven rack tile embeddings serve as queries $\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{rack}} \mathbf{W}_Q$, and the spatial board tokens serve as keys $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{board}} \mathbf{W}_K$ and values $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{board}} \mathbf{W}_V$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \mathrm{softmax}\left( \frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} \right) \mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
where $d_k = 16$. This cross-attention formulation allows each individual rack tile (such as an unplayed `S' or blank) to dynamically attend to specific board coordinates (such as open triple-word hook lanes or parallel vowel slots), producing a context-dependent synergy embedding.

The attended rack representations are averaged via global pooling and concatenated with the globally pooled board representation. The concatenated vector passes through a multi-layer perceptron with Layer Normalization ($128 \to 64 \to 1$) to produce a predicted scalar equity offset $\hat{V}(s) \in \mathbb{R}$.

\subsection{Distillation and Robust Huber Training}

Training an equity network directly from self-play in Scrabble presents severe convergence challenges due to the heavy-tailed variance of 50-point bingo bonuses and random tile draws. We circumvent this instability by adopting a supervised distillation framework. The network is trained on a curated corpus of 500,000 tournament game positions, annotated with asymptotic leaf evaluations derived from deep Monte Carlo rollouts and KLV2 evaluations.

To prevent occasional triple-triple blowouts ($\ge 150$ points) from distorting network weights during gradient descent, we optimize the parameters using a robust Huber loss with threshold $\delta = 1.0$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\delta(y, \hat{y}) = \begin{cases}
        \frac{1}{2} (y - \hat{y})^2 & \text{for } |y - \hat{y}| \le \delta \\
        \delta \cdot (|y - \hat{y}| - \frac{1}{2} \delta) & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The distilled value network achieves an inference latency of $4.2$ milliseconds on a single GPU and $14.8$ milliseconds on a standard 8-core CPU, making it practical for real-time candidate re-ranking during competitive match play.
