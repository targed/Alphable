\section{Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

To evaluate whether \algo's performance represents a statistically valid strategic advantage or an artifact of tile draw variance, we conduct paired duplicate tournaments across two distinct experimental tiers.

\subsection{Paired Duplicate Tournament Methodology}

In standard tournament Scrabble, tile distribution variance is large: drawing blank tiles ($?$) or high-scoring letters ($S$) exerts substantial influence over individual game scores. To isolate algorithmic decision quality from tile luck, we adopt the Paired Duplicate Tournament methodology with Common Random Numbers (CRN) \citep{olaughlin2006quackle}. Each match consists of a duplicate pair comprising two games (Game A and Game B) played with identical underlying tile bag sequences $\sigma \sim \mathrm{Permutations}(100)$. In Game A, Player 1 moves first from Seat 1 while Player 2 moves second from Seat 2. In Game B, the seating order is inverted: Player 2 moves first with the identical opening rack, and Player 1 moves second. The match score differential is evaluated as the average net margin across both games:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = \frac{(S_{1, A} - S_{2, A}) + (S_{1, B} - S_{2, B})}{2}
\end{equation}
As proven in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_duplicate}, the expected differential satisfies $\E[\Delta_{\mathrm{pair}}] = \mu_1 - \mu_2$, confirming that first-mover initiative and bag draw luck cancel out algebraically.

\subsection{Benchmark 2: 10,000-Game Supercomputer Tournament Against Maximally Tuned MAGPIE}
\label{sec:hpc_benchmark}

When holding the opening turn initiative in Game A, \algo\ Hybrid PIMC achieves a mean score of $425.76$ points compared to $405.28$ points for Maximally Tuned \magpie, extracting a net advantage of $+20.48$ points per game. Conversely, when defending from Seat 2 in Game B, Hybrid PIMC limits Tuned \magpie's opening margin to $+12.18$ points ($421.18$ points for Tuned \magpie\ versus $409.00$ points for Hybrid PIMC). Averaging these complementary margins across the duplicate pair yields a net advantage of $+8.30$ points per pair, or $+4.15$ points per game.

This asymmetric performance profile demonstrates that \algo's neural valuation excels particularly during the opening and early midgame turns. By jointly conditioning on board topology and rack leaves, the neural network identifies opening plays that balance immediate score with vowel-consonant flexibility and long-term hook access, converting the first-mover advantage into an average lead of over twenty points. When defending against an opponent's opening play, \algo's negative-evidence belief tracker prevents unnecessary defensive concessions, allowing the engine to minimize opponent scoring without forfeiting positional board equity.

\subsubsection{Convergence Dynamics}

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} and Table~\ref{tab:convergence_trajectory} document the convergence trajectory across sample milestones. In the initial $N = 250$ pairs, sample variance produced an elevated mean spread of $+10.85$ points. As the tournament expanded through $1,000$, $2,500$, and $5,000$ pairs, the observed net spread converged toward its asymptotic value of $+4.15$ points per game, while the paired $t$-statistic grew monotonically from $+3.29$ to $+5.55$. The strictly positive lower bound of the $95\%$ confidence interval ($+2.68$ points) demonstrates that the empirical advantage remains positive regardless of seed sequence.
