\begin{abstract}
Imperfect-information games present a profound challenge for artificial intelligence due to hidden private information, massive combinatorial branching, and the strategic failure of perfect-information approximations known as the strategy fusion flaw. In this work, we present \algo, an open-source, championship-caliber Scrabble engine that combines high-throughput C-based move generation with deep cross-attention value networks, Bayesian belief tracking over hidden opponent racks, and Belief-Augmented Monte Carlo Search (\bams). In rigorous paired duplicate tournament benchmarks comprising 10,000 games (5,000 duplicate pairs), \algo\ achieves a statistically significant victory over 1-ply Static KLV Base \magpie, registering an empirical net point spread of $+7.06 \pm 0.81$ points per game ($p = 2.90 \times 10^{-18}$, paired $t = 8.749$) and a match win rate of $55.63\%$ (Wilson 95\% CI: $[54.25\%, 57.00\%]$). We perform an adversarial parameter audit demonstrating that while \algo\ definitively overcomes static greedy heuristics through compound late-game equity maximization ($+17.67$ net points in pre-endgame and endgame play), deep Monte Carlo simulation rollouts in native C at $\sim 98,000$ rollouts/second retain a substantial lookahead advantage. We formulate the game-theoretic foundations of paired duplicate evaluation, prove variance reduction under Common Random Numbers via the Harris-FKG inequality, and release the complete codebase, evaluation suites, and historical datasets to establish a reproducible benchmark for imperfect-information game AI.
\end{abstract}
