\begin{abstract}
Imperfect-information games with vast combinatorial action spaces present a longstanding challenge for artificial intelligence. In tournament Scrabble, players face hidden private racks, stochastic bag replenishment, and thousands of legal candidate placements on every turn. Existing tournament programs rely on compiled Perfect Information Monte Carlo (PIMC) rollouts or static heuristic lookup tables, both of which suffer from the strategy fusion flaw and decoupled board-rack valuation. In this work, we present \algo, an open-source Scrabble engine that couples high-throughput C move generation with deep cross-attention value networks, Bayesian negative-evidence belief tracking over hidden opponent racks, and Belief-Augmented Monte Carlo Search (\bams). In paired duplicate benchmarks comprising 10,000 games (5,000 duplicate pairs), 2-ply \bams\ outperforms 1-ply static heuristic \magpie\ by $+7.06 \pm 0.81$ points per game ($p = 2.90 \times 10^{-18}$, paired $t = 8.75$). When evaluated against Maximally Tuned Base \magpie\ equipped with compiled PIMC simulation at $\sim 98,000$ rollouts per second, positional pattern terms, and exact endgame solvers, exploratory small-sample matches exhibited high sample variance ($\sigma \approx 105.7$ points), demonstrating the necessity of large-scale testing. To combine deep spatial valuation with asymptotic simulation depth, we introduce \algo\ Hybrid PIMC, which fuses compiled simulation rollouts with cross-attention candidate re-ranking and Bayesian belief tracking. Across a 10,000-game (5,000 duplicate pairs) confirmatory tournament executed on the Missouri S\&T HPC cluster, \algo\ Hybrid PIMC defeated Maximally Tuned \magpie\ with a win rate of $53.30\%$ and an average net spread of $+4.15$ points per game ($95\%$ CI: $[+2.68, +5.61]$ points, total net $+41,474$ points, paired $t = 5.5517$, $p = 2.83 \times 10^{-8}$), establishing a statistically verified 5.55-sigma strategic advantage. We provide mathematical variance-reduction proofs under Common Random Numbers via the Harris-FKG inequality, analyze opening initiative and defensive play, and release the complete codebase, model weights, and match logs under the GPLv3 license.
\end{abstract}
