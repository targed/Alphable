\begin{abstract}
Imperfect-information games with massive action spaces present a classic challenge for artificial intelligence. In tournament Scrabble, agents face hidden private racks, stochastic bag replenishment, and thousands of legal candidate placements per turn. Classical tournament engines rely on compiled Perfect Information Monte Carlo (PIMC) simulation or static heuristic lookup tables, both of which suffer from the strategy fusion flaw and decoupled board-rack valuation. In this work, we present \algo, an open-source Scrabble engine integrating high-throughput C move generation with deep cross-attention value networks, Bayesian negative-evidence belief tracking over hidden opponent racks, and Belief-Augmented Monte Carlo Search (\bams). In paired duplicate benchmarks of 10,000 games (5,000 duplicate pairs), 2-ply \bams\ outperforms 1-ply static heuristic \magpie\ by $+7.06 \pm 0.81$ points per game ($p = 2.90 \times 10^{-18}$, paired $t = 8.75$). 

When audited against Maximally Tuned Base \magpie\ equipped with compiled PIMC simulation at $\sim 98,000$ rollouts/second, positional pattern terms, and exact endgame solvers, exploratory small-sample probes ($N = 40$ games) exhibited high sample variance ($\sigma \approx 105.7$ points), leading to misleading swings. To combine deep valuation with high-speed simulation, we engineered \algo\ Hybrid PIMC, which fuses compiled Monte Carlo simulation with neural candidate re-ranking and Bayesian belief inference. In a 9,998-game (4,999 duplicate pairs) confirmatory tournament executed on the Missouri S\&T HPC cluster across 24 AMD EPYC cores, \algo\ Hybrid PIMC defeated Maximally Tuned \magpie\ with a win rate of $53.30\%$ (2,652 wins, 2,322 losses, 25 ties) and an average net spread of $+4.15$ points per game ($95\%$ CI: $[+2.68, +5.61]$ points, total net $+41,474$ points). A paired Student's $t$-test yields $t = 5.5517$ ($p = 2.83 \times 10^{-8}$), exceeding the 5.5-sigma discovery threshold. We provide mathematical variance-reduction proofs under Common Random Numbers via the Harris-FKG inequality, analyze first-player initiative (+20.48 points first, $-12.18$ points second), and release the codebase, weights, and match logs under the GPLv3 license.
\end{abstract}
